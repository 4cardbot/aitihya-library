## Build a modern Indian luxury textile house for the global home

Not a broad handicraft marketplace. Not a fashion label. Not an “artisan welfare” brand.

Build a house that creates collectible, contemporary textiles for private residences, second homes, boutique hospitality and design-led interiors.

Positioning:

> Indian textile culture, edited into heirlooms for the world’s most discerning homes.

### Why this direction

India’s wealth and luxury-home markets are expanding. Knight Frank counted 85,698 Indian individuals with wealth above US$10 million in 2024, while CBRE reported an 85% year-on-year rise in luxury-home sales in India during H1 2025. Kotak’s luxury index also shows luxury real estate growing 10.8% annually since 2022. [Knight Frank](https://www.knightfrank.com/site-assets/research/reports/the-wealth-report/the-wealth-report-2025.pdf), [CBRE](https://www.cbre.co.in/press-releases/luxury-housing-sales-record-85-yoy), [Kotak Private](https://private.kotak.bank.in/kotak-private-luxury-index-2025.php)

At the global high end, the top 0.1% of luxury customers reportedly account for 37% of market value and expect personalisation, intimacy, excellence and experience—not just expensive objects. [Altagamma](https://altagamma.it/en/studi-e-ricerche/)

India already has an export ecosystem for bed linen, table linen, cushions, curtains, upholstery and other home textiles. The Ministry of Textiles identifies the USA, UK and UAE as important markets, while EPCH recorded 28.83% dollar growth in handprinted-textile exports in FY2024–25. [Ministry of Textiles](https://texmin.nic.in/sites/default/files/MOT%202024-25%20English%20Report%2012.03.2025.pdf), [EPCH](https://epch.in/sites/default/files/electionofcoa/EPCH_Annual_Report_2024-25.pdf)

The opportunity is therefore not “selling Indian craft.” It is converting exceptional craft knowledge into a recognisable luxury product system.

### The product architecture

Start with three craft families, not twenty. Each should have a distinct visual and material language.

**Core collection**

- Quilts, coverlets and bed throws
- Tablecloths, runners and hosting sets
- Cushions, panels and smaller textile objects

**Collector editions**

- One-of-one wall textiles
- Rare techniques from endangered or remote communities
- Numbered collaborations with artists, architects or designers

**Private commissions**

- Complete textile programs for residences, villas and boutique hotels
- Bespoke sizing, colourways and installations

The endangered crafts should be the halo, not the volume engine. They may be too fragile, slow or capacity-constrained to carry the company’s core revenue.

Indicative test price bands:

- ₹25,000–₹75,000: entry objects and table pieces
- ₹75,000–₹3 lakh: throws, quilts and core textiles
- ₹3–12 lakh: large signature pieces and room sets
- ₹12 lakh+: private commissions and collectible works

These are testing bands, not final prices.

### Why not fashion first?

Fashion is attractive but crowded, seasonal, inventory-heavy and dependent on a designer’s personal relevance. Raw Mango already occupies a strong textile-led fashion position, while Good Earth owns a broad Indian lifestyle/home position and Jaipur Rugs has built a large global rug platform. [Raw Mango](https://rawmango.com/pages/raw-mango), [Good Earth](https://www.goodearth.in/collection/jodhpur_577/), [Jaipur Rugs](https://trade.jaipurrugs.com/)

Your strongest defensible territory is narrower:

> rare Indian textile knowledge + contemporary design + luxury interiors + documented provenance.

Home textiles also offer better operational advantages:

- No fit-related returns
- Easier global shipping than furniture
- Longer product life
- Repeat purchases across rooms and homes
- Access to architects, interior designers and hospitality
- Less dependence on fashion seasons
- More scope for customisation without changing the entire product

### Target customer

Do not target “the top 1%” as one audience. Target the design-literate slice within it:

- Indian entrepreneurs and family-office households
- Owners of multiple residences or holiday homes
- Art, design and architecture collectors
- NRIs building homes in India
- Interior designers serving UHNW clients
- Boutique hotels, villas, yachts and private clubs

Visa’s 2025–26 affluent India study reports strong cross-border spending and growing interest in limited-edition and gated collections. That supports private previews, collector circles and invitation-only drops rather than mass digital advertising. [Visa](https://www.visa.co.in/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases/visa-reveals-a-new-premium-spending-playbook-in-affluent-india.html)

### Distribution

Use a dual-engine model:

1. **Brand engine:** private clients, direct online sales, appointments, salons and limited drops.
2. **Trade engine:** architects, interior designers, boutique hospitality and luxury real-estate collaborations.

Start in:

- Mumbai
- Delhi NCR
- Bengaluru
- Hyderabad
- Ahmedabad and Surat
- Dubai and Abu Dhabi

Then test London, New York, Los Angeles and Miami through designers, galleries and private trunk shows—not permanent stores.

Your first physical space should be a textile library and appointment studio, not a conventional retail store.

### What makes it scalable

Your personal artisan knowledge is an extraordinary starting advantage, but it cannot remain inside your head.

Build these assets from day one:

- A documented craft and artisan atlas
- Long-term contracts with selected workshops
- Approved master samples
- Standardised yarn, dye and finishing specifications
- Batch-level maker and material provenance
- Quality-control and rejection procedures
- Export-ready labelling and care documentation
- A designer sample library and specification system
- A private-client CRM

The brand must eventually work even when you are not personally present in every sourcing conversation. Your role should become Chief Curator and Cultural Director, supported by a strong product and operations lead.

### Exit strategy

A 3–4 year sale is ambitious but possible if we build a sellable platform rather than a founder-dependent craft studio.

An ambitious internal Year-4 hurdle would be:

- ₹25–40 crore revenue run-rate
- More than 60% blended gross margin
- Majority revenue from repeatable, carry-forward products
- Positive EBITDA or clear contribution profitability
- 25%+ repeat, referral or private-client revenue
- No single artisan cluster responsible for more than 25% of sales
- Strong full-price sell-through
- Clean IP, contracts, provenance and export documentation

Potential strategic buyers could include large Indian lifestyle platforms such as Reliance, which operates a major luxury and home portfolio, or ABFRL, which is building a portfolio of Indian designer and artisanal brands. [Reliance Brands](https://rcareers.ril.com/sap/bc/bsp/sap/zerec_brands/), [ABFRL](https://www.adityabirla.com/media/stories/the-abfrl-demerger-two-paths-one-vision/)

The buyer will not pay primarily for a beautiful artisan story. It will pay for:

- Brand desirability
- Repeatable product economics
- Exportable distribution
- Proprietary design language
- Reliable supply
- Customer data
- Provenance and cultural credibility

### Our first 90 days

1. Select three craft families using a strict scorecard: quality, capacity, repeatability, lead time, material access and cultural integrity.
2. Develop 20–25 prototypes across bed, table and decorative textiles.
3. Test them with 20 affluent households, 15 interior designers and 5 hospitality buyers.
4. Ask for deposits or pre-orders, not compliments.
5. Build the provenance, care and export documentation simultaneously.
6. Hold private previews in Mumbai and Delhi, followed by a Dubai presentation.
7. Kill any product that receives admiration but cannot command the intended price.

My strongest recommendation is therefore:

> Build a research-led Indian luxury home-textile house, with collectible craft as its halo and repeatable soft furnishings as its commercial engine.

## Competitor businesses globally

I would divide the competitive landscape into four groups.

## 1. Closest Indian competitors

| Business | What they own | Why they matter |
|---|---|---|
| [Good Earth](https://www.goodearth.in/) | Indian luxury lifestyle, home, décor and apparel | Closest benchmark for wealthy Indian consumers, visual language and international retail |
| [Obeetee](https://www.obeetee.in/) | Luxury Indian rugs, furniture, cushions and bedding | Very close to our proposed home-textile direction; already serves designers, hotels and global customers |
| [Jaipur Rugs](https://trade.jaipurrugs.com/) | Handmade rugs and artisan-network scale | Major proof that Indian craft can become a global design business; it reports 40,000+ artisans and 45+ countries |
| [Raw Mango](https://rawmango.com/pages/raw-mango) | Designer-led Indian handloom and textile culture | Strongest benchmark for research-led textile authorship and contemporary Indian luxury |
| [11.11 / eleven eleven](https://11-11.in/pages/about-us) | Small-batch, natural-dye, fibre-to-finish Indian textiles | Closest benchmark for traceability, craft integrity and modern storytelling, although primarily fashion-led |
| [Jaypore](https://www.jaypore.com/) | Broad curated Indian artisanal marketplace | A competitor for discovery, gifting and craft-led home products, but less exclusive and more assortment-driven |

The biggest Indian threats are Good Earth, Obeetee and Jaipur Rugs. Obeetee is especially important because it already extends from rugs into cushions, bedding, furniture and bespoke hospitality projects. [Obeetee home](https://www.obeetee.in/)

## 2. Global luxury linen and home-textile houses

| Business | Core strength |
|---|---|
| [Frette](https://www.frette.com/en_US/homepage) | Luxury bed, bath and table linens; strong hotel and hospitality credibility |
| [SFERRA](https://www.sferra.com/pages/our-story) | Italian-woven linens, rare fibres, exceptional finishing and monogramming |
| [Matouk](https://www.matouk.com/inside-matouk) | American luxury linens, customization, monogramming and hospitality |
| [Tekla](https://teklafabrics.com/about-tekla) | Modern, minimalist, digitally native bedding and home textiles |
| [Loro Piana Interiors](https://www.loropiana.com/interiors/collection-2025/matter/index_EN.php) | Ultra-premium materials such as cashmere, wool and linen for interiors |
| [Mungo](https://mungo.co.za/about-us/) | Transparent, mill-based, heirloom-quality South African textiles |

These brands teach us how to sell ordinary home products—sheets, blankets, towels and throws—as luxury through material quality, service, design discipline and provenance.

Our distinction would be that the luxury comes from Indian technique, regional knowledge and maker provenance rather than Egyptian cotton, Italian weaving or cashmere.

## 3. Decorative and designer textile houses

| Business | Core strength |
|---|---|
| [Pierre Frey](https://www.pierrefrey.com/en/brands/pierre-frey) | French heritage, archives, fabrics, wallpaper, rugs and furniture |
| [Dedar](https://dedar.com/company/about_us/) | Contemporary Italian fabrics, wallcoverings and design collaborations |
| [Rubelli](https://www.rubelli.com/en/) | High-end Italian fabrics, contract interiors and historic archives |
| [de Le Cuona](https://delecuona.com/pages/about-us) | Quiet luxury, natural fibres and bespoke interior tailoring |
| [Élitis](https://elitis.fr/en) | Experimental fabrics, wallcoverings, rugs and technical interior textiles |
| [Jim Thompson Fabrics](https://www.jimthompsonfabrics.com/our-brands/jim-thompson) | Thai silk transformed into an international design and contract business |

These are not always direct consumer competitors. They are important because they own the architect and interior-designer channel. They show how a textile house becomes a specification business through sample libraries, trade accounts, custom sizing, performance standards and hospitality projects.

Jim Thompson is particularly relevant: it turned one national textile heritage—Thai silk—into a global interior-textile platform.

| Business | Core strength |

| [The Rug Company](https://www.therugcompany.com/) | Handmade rugs, designer collaborations, showrooms and bespoke service |

| [La DoubleJ](https://www.ladoublej.com/en/our-story/) | Archive-led Italian prints expanded into fashion, tableware and homeware |

Fortuny, Beni Rugs and The Citizenry are especially useful comparisons because they turn cultural or artisan provenance into a commercial brand rather than selling craft only as a social-impact story.

## Where our opportunity sits

From the public portfolios, the white space appears to be:

> A high-luxury, home-focused Indian textile house that combines the cultural depth of Raw Mango and 11.11, the home authority of Good Earth and Obeetee, and the trade sophistication of Pierre Frey or de Le Cuona.

We should be:

- narrower and more exclusive than Good Earth or Jaypore;
- broader than a rug-only company;
- more interior-focused than Raw Mango or 11.11;
- more culturally specific than Frette or SFERRA;
- more luxurious and design-led than most global artisan marketplaces.

The four brands I would study most closely are:

1. **Good Earth** — Indian luxury positioning and retail.
2. **Obeetee** — Indian craft scaled into home and hospitality.
3. **de Le Cuona** — bespoke textile-house economics and trade relationships.
4. **Fortuny** — how rare textile techniques become collectible luxury.
