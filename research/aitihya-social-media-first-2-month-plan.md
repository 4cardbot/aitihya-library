# Aitihya: first two months social-media plan

**Duration:** 8 weeks  
**Role of social:** editorial showroom, trust layer and qualified-introduction engine.  
**Primary audience:** luxury residential designers and architects, followed by private clients, collectors, galleries and selected hospitality partners.

## 1. What social media must do

Social media should not attempt to sell a ₹2–6 lakh textile panel directly from a feed.

It should:

1. Make Aitihya recognisable.
2. Make the material and technique desirable.
3. Make the provenance credible.
4. Give a designer a reason to request the material library.
5. Give a private client a reason to book a viewing.
6. Create reusable footage for the website, Pramāṇa Story Pages, trade portal, email and press.

The desired journey is:

```text
Instagram / Pinterest / YouTube / referral
          ↓
Collection, film or Pramāṇa Story Page
          ↓
Material-library request or private appointment
          ↓
Trade conversation / room brief
          ↓
Physical sample
          ↓
Quote and deposit
```

## 2. Audience and message by segment

| Audience | What they need to believe | Message | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Luxury interior designer | Aitihya is beautiful and dependable enough to specify | Contemporary Indian textile knowledge, clear dimensions, samples and lead times | Request material library |
| Architect/specifier | The product can fit a real project and meet technical needs | Measured drawings, material records, installation and technical pathway | Open a trade account |
| Private homeowner/family office | The object is rare, meaningful and worth a private appointment | A textile with a specific material, maker and place story | Book private viewing |
| Collector | The edition is authentic, finite and documented | Numbered work, condition, provenance and future care | Request edition dossier |
| Gallery/cultural institution | The work has intellectual and visual depth | Living textile knowledge with rigorous documentation | Request media/archive pack |
| Boutique hospitality | The product creates an experience and can be delivered | Tactile design with a managed project and service pathway | Discuss project brief |

## 3. Channel plan

### Instagram — priority 1

Use Instagram as the daily visual doorway:

- macro material films;
- quiet room imagery;
- process fragments;
- Anjan’s short field notes;
- Pramāṇa proof moments;
- private salon invitations;
- designer-approved installations.

Primary CTAs: “Request the material library,” “Book a private viewing” and “Watch the complete story.”

### YouTube or branded video archive — priority 1

Use for:

- 3–8 minute cinematic documentaries;
- 60–90 second collection films;
- technique explainers;
- designer conversations;
- care and conservation films.

Use long-form videos as the canonical source, then cut them into vertical edits. Measure retention, completion and traffic to the Story Page rather than chasing views alone. [YouTube Analytics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9002587?hl=en)

### Pinterest — priority 1

Create boards for:

- material palettes;
- contemporary rooms;
- wall textile scale;
- quiet luxury Indian interiors;
- natural dyes and fibre;
- collection chapters;
- designer project references.

Pinterest is relevant because users search, save and shop visual ideas. Use precise titles, descriptions, dimensions, materials and links to product/collection pages. [Pinterest Business](https://business.pinterest.com/en-us/how-pinterest-works/)

### LinkedIn — priority 2

Use for the professional ecosystem:

- designer and architect relationships;
- project case studies;
- technical and acoustic collaborations;
- hospitality references;
- hiring and partnerships;
- supply-chain and documentation milestones.

Do not republish every Instagram post. LinkedIn should make Aitihya look operationally credible.

### Email and private messaging — priority 1 for conversion

Use permission-based communication for:

- private collection previews;
- sample-library follow-up;
- appointment confirmations;
- edition reservations;
- care and ownership messages;
- personal introductions.

No mass promotional broadcasts, discount codes or uninvited luxury spam.

## 4. Brand rules for every post

### Visual rules

- Use ecru, bone, charcoal, slate, madder, mineral and natural-dye tones.
- Use architectural compositions, negative space and tactile close-ups.
- Let the hand, fibre and edge occupy the frame.
- Use natural or controlled directional light.
- Keep camera movement slow and observational.
- Show the object in a contemporary interior without over-styling it.
- Use the Aitihya wordmark consistently; keep `AITIHYA` as the practical ASCII fallback in URLs and metadata.

### Language rules

- Be precise about what is documented.
- Name makers and ateliers only with permission.
- Use region-level geography publicly unless exact detail is approved.
- Explain handmade variation honestly.
- Separate traditional cultural knowledge from new Aitihya design work.
- Avoid poverty framing, “exotic India,” “saving the artisan,” “last living master” and unsupported sustainability claims.
- Never make a claim merely because it sounds luxurious.

### Post structure

Every post should have:

1. one clear visual idea;
2. one useful or emotionally resonant sentence;
3. one specific CTA;
4. one destination link or tracked profile action;
5. approved attribution and rights record.

## 5. Content production system

One field visit or studio shoot should produce:

- one 3–8 minute documentary;
- one 60–90 second collection film;
- six to ten vertical edits;
- twenty to thirty stills;
- one field note;
- one technique explainer;
- captions and transcript;
- product-page copy;
- a Pramāṇa Story Page;
- a designer/trade education sheet.

### Shot list

- raw fibre and yarn;
- hands preparing or manipulating material;
- loom/tool/process details;
- rhythm, sound and movement of work;
- approved landscape or regional context;
- quality and finishing inspection;
- the completed object at room scale;
- care/repair detail;
- maker/atelier interview where consented.

### Consent checklist

Before publication, confirm consent for:

- face and voice;
- name and attribution;
- workshop and location;
- process and cultural information;
- archive images;
- music;
- client room and installation;
- future edits and international distribution.

Store consent and rights records in Pramāṇa’s media archive.

## 6. Eight-week operating calendar

### Week 1 — quiet introduction: what Aitihya is

**Objective:** establish the world without selling aggressively.

**Instagram**

- Post 1: Aitihya wordmark on a quiet architectural background.
- Post 2: macro textile detail with a short statement about living textile knowledge.
- Reel: 15–20 second slow material montage.
- Stories: founder introduction, material question, appointment waitlist.

**Pinterest**

- Create boards: “Aitihya Materials,” “Exceptional Rooms,” “Textile Wall Objects.”
- Publish 8–10 pins from approved mood and material imagery.

**LinkedIn**

- Founder/company post explaining the designer-first proposition.

**Email**

- One private note to warm contacts: “We are building a new Indian textile house for exceptional interiors.”

**Sales action**

- Add 50 priority design accounts to the CRM.
- Record introducer, city, project type and next action.

**Success evidence**

- profile visits from relevant designers;
- replies from warm contacts;
- first material-library requests;
- no need to optimise for follower count.

### Week 2 — material before product

**Objective:** make the material feel desirable before revealing the catalogue.

**Instagram**

- Carousel: fibre/yarn/process with plain-language captions.
- Reel: hand, tension, texture and sound of making.
- Still: a material palette with one line about why the palette was chosen.
- Stories: poll for room applications; question box for designers.

**Pinterest**

- Publish 10–12 material and room pins with searchable descriptions.

**YouTube**

- Upload a 45–60 second material study.

**LinkedIn**

- Short post on how a designer will receive samples and specifications.

**Sales action**

- Personally send the material study to 10 priority designers with a one-line question about their current projects.

**CTA**

> “Request the first Aitihya material library.”

### Week 3 — technique and living knowledge

**Objective:** establish authority without turning the maker into a prop.

**Instagram**

- Reel: 30–45 second technique fragment.
- Carousel: what the technique does materially, not only historically.
- Portrait or hands image only with consent.
- Stories: Anjan answers three questions about the technique.

**YouTube**

- Publish a 2–3 minute technique explainer or interview excerpt.

**Pinterest**

- Create one technique board and link each pin to a structured journal page.

**Sales action**

- Invite 10 designers to a 20-minute material review call.

**CTA**

> “Book a private material review.”

### Week 4 — Pramāṇa and proof

**Objective:** show that the brand’s provenance is evidence-based.

**Instagram**

- Reel: demonstration of scanning a product QR.
- Carousel: what a P1/P2/P3 Pramāṇa record means.
- Still: certificate, material sample and object together.

**YouTube**

- Behind-the-scenes edit showing the documentary and product record being connected.

**Pinterest**

- Pins linking material, technique and proof pages.

**Email**

- Private note explaining provenance without using legalistic language.

**Sales action**

- Send a private sample of the Story Page to priority designers and collectors.

**CTA**

> “Request the edition dossier” or “Open a trade account.”

### Week 5 — first object reveal

**Objective:** move from world-building to product desire.

**Instagram**

- Hero reveal of the first wall textile or signature throw.
- Detail Reel showing edge, hand and scale.
- Carousel with dimensions, material, lead time and intended room.
- Stories: appointment slots and edition waitlist.

**Pinterest**

- Build a room board around the object with material and scale references.

**LinkedIn**

- Designer-facing post with specification and sample information.

**Email**

- Collection preview to approved list only.

**Sales action**

- Open appointment calendar.
- Send five physical or digital sample previews to serious accounts.

**CTA**

> “Book a private preview.”

### Week 6 — room proof and designer validation

**Objective:** show how the product behaves in a real interior.

**Instagram**

- Room mockup or installation image.
- Designer conversation: why the object was specified.
- Short video on scale, placement and care.

**Pinterest**

- Publish room views, detail crops and colour pairings.

**YouTube**

- Release the full 3–8 minute object documentary.

**LinkedIn**

- Case-study post focused on specification, lead time and collaboration.

**Sales action**

- Run the first private designer presentation or salon.
- Capture briefs, objections and price feedback in CRM.

**CTA**

> “Request the material library for your next room.”

### Week 7 — private release and conversion

**Objective:** convert the warmest audience into deposits and paid pilots.

**Instagram**

- Documentary trailer.
- Edition number and availability post.
- Story sequence showing the path from object to certificate to room.

**Paid media**

- Retarget meaningful video viewers, Story Page visitors and appointment-page visitors.
- Test a small designer/interiors audience in the active market.
- Do not broaden targeting yet.

**Email/private messaging**

- Personal reservation note to qualified collectors and designers.

**Sales action**

- Follow up every open brief.
- Issue quotes with deposit terms and lead times.

**CTA**

> “Reserve an edition” or “Discuss a project brief.”

### Week 8 — care, continuity and review

**Objective:** close the first cycle with trust and a clear next action.

**Instagram**

- Care and conservation post.
- Repair/continuity Reel.
- Founder note on what was learned from the first release.

**YouTube**

- Publish a shorter “how to live with this textile” film or cutdown.

**Pinterest**

- Create a care and long-term-living board.

**Email**

- Send a concise editorial recap with appointment link and next preview date.

**Sales action**

- Review every account, quote, sample and deposit.
- Identify the three strongest product signals and the three strongest objections.

**CTA**

> “Book the next private preview.”

## 7. Weekly publishing template

| Day | Content | Channel | Commercial purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Material/detail still or carousel | Instagram, Pinterest | Recognition and saves |
| Tuesday | Designer/trade note | LinkedIn, email | Professional credibility |
| Wednesday | Short film | Instagram, YouTube Shorts | Discovery and emotional proof |
| Thursday | Story Page/Pramāṇa/care explanation | Instagram Stories, website | Trust and education |
| Friday | Room/context image or appointment invitation | Instagram, Pinterest | Specification and conversion |
| Weekly | 5–10 personal follow-ups | Email/approved messaging | Relationship and sales |

Do not publish every day simply to appear active. Reserve the best material for collection chapters, private previews and product moments.

## 8. Paid media rules for the first two months

### Weeks 1–4

- No broad conversion campaigns.
- Use organic publishing and warm introductions.
- Boost only the strongest material or documentary post if it generates relevant saves, profile visits or designer enquiries.
- Use no discount code.

### Weeks 5–8

- Allocate the majority of paid spend to retargeting engaged video viewers, website visitors and Story Page visitors.
- Allocate a smaller portion to carefully selected interiors/design audiences in Mumbai, Delhi NCR and Dubai.
- Use LinkedIn only for tightly scoped trade tests if the account list and landing page are ready.
- Stop any campaign that generates cheap views but no qualified action.

### Suggested test allocation

As a percentage of the small launch-media budget:

- 60% retargeting and warm audiences;
- 25% controlled discovery around material/documentary films;
- 15% creative and audience experiments.

Production, sample kits and private presentations should receive more investment than media buying during this period.

## 9. Metrics and targets

### Weekly operating dashboard

- qualified design accounts reached;
- meaningful video completion rate;
- repeat viewers;
- profile visits from target geographies;
- Pramāṇa Story Page visits;
- material-library requests;
- trade-account applications;
- appointments booked;
- sample requests and sample status;
- quote requests;
- deposits;
- referrals and earned introductions.

### Eight-week target range

These are operating targets, not forecasts:

- 50 priority design accounts fully recorded in CRM;
- 15 qualified design conversations;
- 8 material-library or sample requests;
- 5 serious appointments;
- 3 project briefs or quotes;
- 1–2 deposits or paid development engagements;
- clear evidence of which content pillar produces qualified action.

If these are not achieved, first revise the proposition, imagery, CTA or audience list. Do not respond by automatically increasing posting frequency or paid spend.

## 10. Governance and response rules

- Every post has an owner and approval status.
- Every maker/client/location/media appearance has a rights record.
- Sensitive cultural or geographic information requires Anjan’s review and, where relevant, community/atelier approval.
- Negative comments about authenticity, attribution or exploitation receive a factual, non-defensive response and are escalated internally.
- Product claims link to Pramāṇa evidence.
- Client enquiries receive a response within one business day during the launch period.
- DMs are recorded in CRM when they create a real commercial relationship.
- No client address, purchase value or private-room image is published without explicit consent.

## 11. Team for the first two months

- **Anjan:** final authority on textile facts, maker relationships and sensitive stories.
- **Brand/editorial lead:** calendar, voice, visual consistency and approvals.
- **Documentary producer/editor:** master film, short edits, captions and archive.
- **Photographer/content assistant:** product, room and process stills.
- **Trade/CRM lead:** designer outreach, sample requests, appointments and follow-up.
- **Paid-media specialist:** limited campaign setup and weekly reporting, part-time or freelance.

Do not hire a generic social-media manager whose only KPI is posting volume. Aitihya needs an editorial and trade-content operator.

## 12. End-of-month reviews

### End of week 4

Ask:

- Which material or technique generated serious designer questions?
- Which post drove sample requests rather than likes?
- Did people understand Pramāṇa?
- Are we showing too much geography or personal information?
- Does the feed already look like a coherent house?

### End of week 8

Ask:

- Which audience converted to appointments and briefs?
- Which film had the strongest meaningful completion and enquiry rate?
- Which product had the strongest request-to-deposit signal?
- Did the social story match the physical sample experience?
- Which accounts deserve a private presentation next month?
- What should be stopped, repeated or redesigned?

## Final decision

Use social media, but protect the brand from becoming a content factory. Aitihya should publish slowly, beautifully and with evidence; use social to earn the next conversation, then let the material library, Pramāṇa record, documentary and human sales process close the relationship.
