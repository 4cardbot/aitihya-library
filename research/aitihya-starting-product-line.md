# Aitihya Collection 01: starting product line

**Decision document**  
**Prepared:** 30 September 2026  
**Brand:** Aitihya  
**Provenance and certification layer:** Pramāṇa  
**Purpose:** define the first commercially testable product line, initial quantities, pricing hypotheses, product specifications and launch gates.

## 1. The decision

Aitihya should begin with one tightly edited interior collection of **12 public designs**:

| Product family | Number of designs | Commercial role |
|---|---:|---|
| Signature cushion covers | 6 | Repeatable core; room completion; the easiest entry to the brand |
| Throws and decorative coverlets | 3 | Larger, tactile purchases; stronger material and craft expression |
| Textile wall panels | 3 | Halo objects; collector interest; designer and architectural specifications |
| Bespoke commissions | By exception | Prestige, learning and high-value project work; not counted as a normal SKU family |

This is the best synthesis of the seven consultant plans:

- Plans 1 and 7 correctly identify interiors as the strongest starting category and propose cushions, throws and wall textiles.
- Plans 2–6 correctly identify the value of a halo-and-engine structure, rare fibres, architectural textiles and private commissions.
- The more aggressive wearable, acoustic, hospitality and archive proposals should be treated as later extensions, not launch commitments.
- The 70/20/10 portfolio logic remains useful: approximately 70% repeatable core, 20% numbered editions and 10% bespoke over time.

The five demo products currently in the software database are **placeholder records for the platform**. They should not be treated as the final 12-design commercial assortment.

## 2. Collection 01 product architecture

### 2.1 The collection idea

Collection 01 should express one coherent Aitihya design language: quiet, tactile, contemporary and recognisably rooted in Indian textile knowledge. It should not look like a catalogue of every region, technique or village Anjan has encountered.

The customer should understand five things quickly:

1. The object is visually distinctive.
2. The material and construction are exceptional.
3. It has a specific place, process and maker history.
4. It belongs in a contemporary room.
5. Aitihya can deliver it professionally.

Anjan’s anthropology is the source of authority. The product itself must remain easy to understand and desire; deeper fieldwork belongs in the Pramāṇa record, cinematic documentary and editorial layer.

### 2.2 Product-layer split

| Layer | Target share | What belongs here | Inventory principle |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Core | 70% | Repeatable cushions, throws and coverlets | Carry-forward designs; controlled variations; small finished stock and made-to-order replenishment |
| Editions | 20% | Numbered wall panels and exceptional textile objects | Small edition or one-of-one; produce against deposits where possible |
| Bespoke | 10% | Custom panels, room groupings and exceptional commissions | Deposit-led development; capped capacity; no open-ended revisions |

## 3. Recommended launch assortment

The following prices are **initial pricing hypotheses**, not validated willingness-to-pay. Indian prices should be tested as MRP inclusive of applicable taxes. US prices are suggested retail prices before local sales tax, duties, shipping and installation. They are not simple currency conversions: they account for market positioning, export overhead, customer service and margin requirements.

| Family | Designs | First physical run | Suggested India price | Suggested US price |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Signature cushions | 6 | 3 finished pieces per design | ₹24,000–₹55,000 each | $500–$1,050 each |
| Throws / decorative coverlets | 3 | 2 finished pieces per design | ₹85,000–₹2,50,000 | $1,800–$5,000 |
| Textile wall panels | 3 | 1 finished master per design | ₹2,50,000–₹12,00,000 | $5,000–$22,000 |
| Bespoke commissions | 2 pilot commissions in Year 1 | Deposit-led; no speculative stock | ₹8,00,000–₹25,00,000+ | $18,000–$60,000+ |

### Recommended price ladder within each family

The collection should not make every item expensive merely because it is handmade. It needs an understandable first purchase and a credible ladder into collector territory.

#### Cushions

- Four core designs: ₹24,000–₹32,000 / $500–$650 each.
- Two signature designs: ₹38,000–₹55,000 / $750–$1,050 each.
- Pairs may be offered, but the customer should be able to buy a single cushion. Do not force pair purchases before demand is understood.

#### Throws and coverlets

- Repeatable core throw: ₹85,000–₹1,25,000 / $1,800–$2,600.
- Rare-fibre or more complex construction: ₹1,35,000–₹1,85,000 / $2,800–$3,800.
- Numbered collector coverlet: ₹2,00,000–₹2,50,000 / $4,000–$5,000.

#### Wall panels

- Contemporary repeatable edition: ₹2,50,000–₹4,50,000 / $5,000–$8,500.
- Complex surface-work edition: ₹5,00,000–₹7,50,000 / $9,000–$14,000.
- Archival or one-of-one halo panel: ₹8,00,000–₹12,00,000 / $16,000–$22,000.

The upper end should be reserved for genuine scale, scarcity, fibre value, labour complexity, documentation and finishing. A high price without a visibly exceptional object will damage trust.

## 4. Product family specifications

## 4.1 Signature cushion covers — six designs

### Role

Cushions are the entry point into Aitihya’s interior world. They allow designers to specify several pieces in one room, let a private client start with one object, and give the company a repeatable product on which to learn quality control, photography, packaging, export labelling and reorders.

### Suggested six-design structure

| Design slot | Working product idea | Material / technique direction | Commercial intent |
|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | Quiet field | Handspun cotton, Kala cotton or fine Eri; restrained structure | Most repeatable core |
| C02 | Woven interruption | Two-tone handloom structure or engineered stripe | Core with a recognisable Aitihya signature |
| C03 | Mark and memory | Embroidery or appliqué based on a co-developed composition | Surface-story design; not a direct reproduction of a community motif |
| C04 | Fine fibre study | Eri, Muga, Pashmina or another verified fine fibre, subject to supply and testing | Material-led premium core |
| C05 | Landscape / archive fragment | More detailed handwork, weaving or regional technique translated into a contemporary layout | Signature design and editorial anchor |
| C06 | Collector cushion | Numbered, more labour-intensive composition or exceptional fibre combination | Small edition; tests collector appetite |

These are design directions, not a decision to commercialise a particular community technique without consultation. Each technique must pass the selection scorecard in Section 8.

### Recommended physical specifications

- Primary sizes: 45 × 45 cm and 45 × 60 cm.
- Offer the cover separately from a high-quality insert initially, unless the export market requires a complete cushion.
- Hidden zip or another repairable closure; the closure should not compromise the hand-made face.
- Reverse in a stable, compatible cloth rather than a cheap generic backing.
- Defined tolerances for dimensions, colour variation, seam alignment, surface irregularity and acceptable hand variation.
- Product-specific care label and fibre disclosure.
- Pramāṇa ID attached to the internal product record and printed as a discreet label or card.
- QR/NFC access to the product record and the cinematic product story; no claim of live GPS tracking.

### What makes a cushion worthy of the premium

The premium must be visible through at least three of these elements:

- an original composition rather than a generic traditional motif;
- exceptional hand-feel or rare verified fibre;
- unusual construction or surface depth;
- disciplined finishing and a strong reverse side;
- a small, documented edition;
- a meaningful maker and process record;
- a professional object file that makes specification easy.

Current official market listings show why Aitihya cannot rely on the broad phrase “Indian craft cushion” alone: Good Earth’s cushion catalogue spans roughly ₹2,400–₹15,000, while an individual embroidered silk cushion is listed at ₹12,500. Aitihya’s proposed ₹24,000–₹55,000 range therefore needs stronger design authorship, finishing, scarcity and service. [Good Earth cushion catalogue](https://www.goodearth.in/catalogue/category/living/bed-bath_194/?category_shop=Home+%3E+Home+Textiles+%3E+Cushion+Covers&sort_by=price_asc&source=plp) and [Jade Garden embroidered cushion](https://www.goodearth.in/catalogue/jade-garden-yasera-emb-cc-sand-18x18-dupion-silk_42059/).

For US positioning, the range is below the highest luxury cashmere-home prices but above ordinary decorative cushions. Loro Piana currently lists cashmere pillows around $875–$1,280, while SAVED New York lists a cashmere pillow at $550. These are useful anchors, not like-for-like comparisons. [Loro Piana Labyrinth pillow](https://us.loropiana.com/en/at-home/textile/plaids-and-cushions/labyrinth-pillow-FAP5793_T1P3.html), [Loro Piana Nanga pillow](https://us.loropiana.com/en/at-home/textile/plaids-and-cushions/nanga-pillow-FAO3350_D014.html) and [SAVED Gabriel pillow](https://saved-ny.com/products/gabriel-pillow).

## 4.2 Throws and decorative coverlets — three designs

### Role

Throws are the strongest bridge between a decorative object and a serious textile. They demonstrate fibre, drape, scale and durability. They can be used on a bed, daybed, sofa or reading chair without requiring a full bedding or upholstery system.

### Suggested three-design structure

| Design slot | Working product idea | Material / technique direction | Commercial intent |
|---|---|---|---|
| T01 | Everyday heirloom | Fine cotton, Kala cotton, Eri or a blended construction with a stable repeat | Repeatable core; highest reorder potential |
| T02 | Rare-fibre field | Verified Pashmina, Muga, Eri or another material with documented composition | Premium material story; private-client and designer use |
| T03 | Numbered coverlet | More complex construction, surface-work or large-scale composition | Edition and collector halo |

### Recommended physical specifications

- Primary size: approximately 140 × 220 cm.
- Secondary size: approximately 160 × 240 cm, made only where the technique and loom capacity support it.
- Use a restrained palette that works with contemporary interiors; avoid too many colourways at launch.
- Include a sample swatch or folded detail image that helps designers judge scale and drape.
- Specify edge finishing, shrinkage allowance, colourfastness expectation and acceptable irregularity before production.
- Quote lead time honestly. A longer lead time can support luxury; an inaccurate lead time destroys trust.
- Default to made-to-order replenishment after the first master and first saleable unit.

### Technology and performance boundary

The first throws should be beautiful domestic objects, not technical performance products. Do not claim acoustic damping, fire rating, stain resistance or commercial upholstery suitability unless the complete material and construction have been tested and the claim is attached to that exact specification.

If a throw is later developed for a daybed, yacht or hospitality setting, create a separate technical record in Pramāṇa for fibre composition, test method, care, installation and intended environment.

Luxury US home listings show a wide premium range: Loro Piana’s official home-textile collection includes throws and plaids reaching several thousand dollars, and SAVED New York lists cashmere throws around $1,850. Aitihya’s proposed $1,800–$5,000 range is therefore plausible only for objects with genuinely strong material, scale, finish and provenance—not for an ordinary throw with a story attached. [Loro Piana home textiles](https://us.loropiana.com/en/at-home/textile) and [SAVED New York home collection](https://saved-ny.com/collections/saved-ny-x-lisa-corti).

## 4.3 Textile wall panels — three designs

### Role

Wall panels are Aitihya’s signature and halo category. They make the brand legible to interior designers, art advisers and collectors and create a path to larger commissions without forcing the company to begin as a gallery or a full wallcovering manufacturer.

### Suggested three-design structure

| Design slot | Working product idea | Material / technique direction | Edition logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| W01 | Contemporary woven geometry | Structured weave, restrained relief or controlled colour field | Edition of approximately 8–12 |
| W02 | Surface-work composition | Embroidery, appliqué, layered textile or other labour-intensive surface | Edition of approximately 3–6 |
| W03 | Archival halo | Rare technique, exceptional fibre, field-researched composition or one-of-one work | One-of-one or edition of up to 3 |

### Recommended physical specifications

- First sizes: approximately 90 × 140 cm, 120 × 180 cm and 150 × 220 cm, depending on the design.
- Provide the textile as a finished object with a considered mounting solution; do not sell a loose textile that leaves the designer to solve installation alone.
- Offer mounting or framing as a separately priced service through a vetted partner.
- Photograph front, reverse, edge, texture and installed scale.
- Include installation instructions, hanging hardware requirements and room-condition guidance.
- Record edition number, dimensions, maker attribution, design version, date, condition and ownership history in Pramāṇa.

### Panel price logic

The panel range should be legible to both trade and private buyers:

- ₹2.5–4.5 lakh / $5,000–$8,500: repeatable collectible edition.
- ₹5–7.5 lakh / $9,000–$14,000: technically or materially complex edition.
- ₹8–12 lakh / $16,000–$22,000: genuine halo work with scarcity, scale or exceptional documentation.

The more ambitious consultant proposals of $10,000–$45,000 can become relevant for larger, one-of-one installations after Aitihya has proved its design language, installation capability and collector demand. They should not define every first panel.

## 4.4 Bespoke commissions — two pilot projects in Year 1

Bespoke work should be accepted selectively and only after the corresponding production capability is understood.

### Pilot 1: private residence or family office

Example scope:

- one library, study or bedroom wall textile;
- a coordinated daybed throw and cushion group;
- custom palette developed from the room’s material board;
- one Pramāṇa dossier covering design development, approved sample and final object.

Indicative starting quote: ₹8–18 lakh / $18,000–$40,000, depending on scale, travel, mounting and complexity.

### Pilot 2: design-led hospitality or architectural project

Example scope:

- a lobby, private dining room, suite or members’ club installation;
- a small number of related panels rather than a full commercial wallcovering system;
- an agreed prototype and one production standard before any larger commitment.

Indicative starting quote: ₹15–25 lakh+ / $35,000–$60,000+, quoted after a technical brief and site review.

### Commercial rules

- Obtain a 50–70% deposit before material procurement and artisan production.
- Define the number of design revisions and sample approvals.
- Separate design fee, production, mounting, installation, freight and taxes in the quotation.
- Do not allow a bespoke project to interrupt committed collection orders without a capacity decision.
- Secure image, usage, attribution and project-publication permissions in writing.

## 5. Initial production quantities

The first batch should create a convincing sample library without locking up too much working capital.

| Product | Calculation | Initial finished pieces |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cushions | 6 designs × 3 pieces | 18 |
| Throws / coverlets | 3 designs × 2 pieces | 6 |
| Wall panels | 3 designs × 1 master | 3 |
| **Total** |  | **27 pieces** |

### How to use the 27 pieces

- One cushion from each design should be reserved as the master/sample-library piece.
- The second cushion can support private presentations, photography and designer appointments.
- The third should be saleable stock where quality permits.
- One throw from each design should be the master/sample piece; the second can be sold or held for a confirmed appointment.
- Each wall panel should be the first finished master and should not be duplicated until there is a deposit, serious collector commitment or a deliberate edition decision.

Do not produce a complete edition in advance. Make one master, validate the object and lead time, then produce against deposits or credible purchase orders.

## 6. What should not be in Collection 01

The following should be explicitly deferred:

- rugs and carpets;
- furniture;
- a full bedding system with sheets, duvets and fitted components;
- fitted apparel and seasonal fashion collections;
- a broad tableware or dining range;
- commercial wallcovering sold by the roll;
- technical acoustic products;
- large upholstery yardage;
- a multi-country retail launch;
- a broad marketplace representing every craft tradition Anjan knows.

Rugs may become a later halo category, but they bring freight, installation, sampling, room-scale photography and strong international competition. Beni Rugs’ official custom range, for example, starts in the hundreds of dollars and individual custom products can be listed around $930, so Aitihya would need a genuinely differentiated art-and-design proposition rather than simply a handmade rug story. [Beni Rugs custom collection](https://www.benirugs.com/collections/all-customizable) and [example custom rug](https://www.benirugs.com/products/003-natural-wool-midnight-black).

## 7. Wearables: a controlled experiment, not a launch pillar

Several consultants proposed a 30–40% wearable component because wraps and capes are compact, sizing-agnostic and connected to Anjan’s textile expertise. Other plans, especially the most operationally grounded launch recommendation, correctly warn that apparel introduces a second brand language and additional product-development complexity.

The resolution is:

- no public wearable range in Collection 01;
- one private prototype of an oversized wrap or cape;
- maximum 10–20 units only if material, finishing and demand justify it;
- test price: approximately ₹1,20,000–₹2,25,000 / $2,500–$4,500;
- launch in Collection 02 only if at least five qualified buyers or designers request it and at least two place deposits.

This preserves the option without diluting Aitihya’s first product world.

## 8. Craft and supplier selection scorecard

Choose two core craft families and one halo technique for Collection 01. A technique should not be selected only because it is rare or endangered.

Score each candidate from 1–5 and record the evidence:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Visual distinctiveness | Is the work recognisable in a contemporary room without a long explanation? |
| Product fit | Does it work structurally as a cushion, throw or panel? |
| Repeatability | Can the intended quantity be made without damaging the technique or community? |
| Quality control | Can tolerances, colour, finishing and defects be defined? |
| Capacity | Can the workshop meet realistic lead times and communicate constraints? |
| Economics | Does it reach the target price after fair artisan payment, rework, QC and selling costs? |
| Export suitability | Can fibre, care, origin, packaging and shipping requirements be documented? |
| Community governance | Are attribution, consent, compensation, benefit-sharing and design rights clear? |
| Repairability | Can the object be repaired, conserved or responsibly retired? |
| Founder independence | Can the knowledge be documented and taught to a team over time? |

Any candidate scoring below 3 on capacity, quality control, community governance or economics should not carry a launch commitment without a mitigation plan.

## 9. Pramāṇa record requirements for every product

Pramāṇa should create trust and useful after-sales service, not become a decorative blockchain exercise.

Every Aitihya product record should contain, where applicable:

- unique product ID and QR code;
- optional NFC tag for a more discreet physical interaction;
- product name, family, design version and edition number;
- dimensions and weight;
- fibre and material composition;
- technique and process description;
- maker, workshop or community attribution, with permission status;
- production date and location at the level the maker has approved;
- approved master sample and QC status;
- care, cleaning and storage instructions;
- repairs, conservation or ownership transfers;
- documentary photography and the cinematic product story;
- the exact claims Pramāṇa verifies and the claims it does not verify.

### Tagging principle

Use a QR code as the default because it is inexpensive, visible and works across markets. Add NFC to higher-value panels, rare-fibre pieces and bespoke commissions when the cost and durability make sense.

Do not attach a live GPS tracker to ordinary cushions, throws or artisan production. GPS raises privacy, battery, durability, customs and security issues and does not create meaningful customer value for these products. If location is important, record approved origin and production information in the Pramāṇa registry rather than tracking a person or object continuously.

## 10. Product story and digital experience

Scanning the product QR should open a calm, mobile-first Pramāṇa page, not a generic product listing. The page should contain:

1. a short cinematic documentary about the specific product or design;
2. the object’s material, process and dimensions;
3. the human story, with attribution and consent;
4. the edition and ownership record where relevant;
5. care and repair guidance;
6. a request for a private consultation, replacement or provenance transfer.

The documentary should be personalised to the product: raw material, hands, loom or workshop, detail shots, finishing and the final object in a contemporary room. It should avoid poverty imagery, exoticising language and claims that cannot be substantiated.

The product page should continue to work if the video is unavailable. The evidence record is the durable layer; the film is the emotional layer.

## 11. Validation gates before scaling

Collection 01 should scale only when it produces paid evidence.

### Buyer validation

- 15 Indian luxury residential designer interviews completed.
- 5 interviews across the US, UK and UAE completed.
- At least 5 qualified design studios request samples, specifications or a proposal.
- At least 3 unrelated buying organisations place paid pilot orders or deposits.
- At least 2 buyers accept the proposed price without a major discount.

### Product validation

- At least 3 cushion designs receive paid demand.
- At least 2 throw designs receive paid demand.
- At least 1 wall panel receives a serious paid commitment.
- The same core design can be reproduced within documented tolerances.
- Actual artisan pay, time, wastage, rework, finishing, QC, packaging, freight, commission and claims are included in the cost model.

### Kill rules

Pause or redesign a product that receives admiration but no deposit at a sustainable price. Do not keep it alive because it has a compelling story, is technically rare or was expensive to prototype.

## 12. Immediate next actions

1. Select the two core craft families and one halo technique using the scorecard.
2. Name the 12 designs only after the material and visual language are coherent.
3. Produce prototypes for all six cushions, three throws and three panels, but do not commit to full editions.
4. Create cost sheets and Pramāṇa records while the prototypes are being made.
5. Photograph the products in a consistent Aitihya room language.
6. Produce the first three cinematic product stories for the strongest prototypes.
7. Present the sample library privately to designers and qualified collectors.
8. Revise price, dimensions, lead time and production method using deposits and feedback.
